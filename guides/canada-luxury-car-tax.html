What does a $100,000 car really cost?

Canada’s federal luxury tax can change the math on a new vehicle. Here is the high-level explanation, with the official CRA source linked for the details and exceptions.

Published September 12, 2026 · Updated September 23, 2026

The luxury tax is only one part of the purchase decision. Provincial sales tax, registration, insurance, financing, dealer fees and ownership costs still need to be considered separately.

The federal threshold

The Canada Revenue Agency says the luxury tax generally applies to a qualifying “subject vehicle” priced or valued above the $100,000 price threshold, unless an exception applies.

How the broad calculation works

For an eligible subject vehicle, CRA describes the tax as the lesser of:

  • 10% of the taxable amount; or
  • 20% of the amount above the $100,000 threshold.

The exact taxable amount and whether an exception applies depend on the transaction. A dealership’s invoice and the official CRA guidance should control the final answer.

Questions to ask the dealer

  • Is this vehicle a subject vehicle under the federal rules?
  • Is the displayed price before or after the luxury tax?
  • Which amount is being used for the tax calculation?
  • Are dealer fees, accessories or improvements changing the taxable amount?
  • What provincial taxes and registration costs are separate?
Official source: Read the CRA guidance on subject vehicles. Tax rules can change; this page is educational and is not tax or legal advice.
